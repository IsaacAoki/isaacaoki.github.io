Sheet 03

What the helix does as you scroll

One continuous object, five states. It is turning in every one of them — the scroll changes what it is, never whether it moves.

Zone 01 · DNA

The template

Both backbones present, tightly wound, base pairs at full contrast. This is the densest the drawing ever gets — matching documents nobody outside the trial will ever read.

Protocols · SAPs · Investigator brochures · Consent forms

Zone 02 · Transcription

The strands part

Separation widens with scroll depth; the rust backbone fades out of the drawing entirely. Base pairs unhook and dim. Nothing else on the page changes — the transition belongs to the helix alone.

Transition band · no work items sit here

Zone 03 · RNA

The transcript

A single backbone, still turning, bases now unpaired and pointing outward. Each unpaired base is a published piece — readable, but only by machinery that already speaks the language.

CSRs · Manuscripts · Abstracts · Posters

Zone 04 · Translation

Read out, in another language

The strand feeds through a fixed reader while the chain emerges below it. The one moment on the page where something is held still — everything else keeps turning around it. This is where the before/after rewrite lives.

Plain-language rewrite, shown side by side

Zone 05 · Protein

The functional product

No helix left — a folded chain, tumbling on its own axis. The rotation carries through to the end of the page, which is the point: the turning was never decoration, it was the subject.

Lay summaries · Decision aids · Patient materials
